Murder Investigation / Utah

Mackenzie Lueck: The 3 A.M. Meeting

Open full case file →

At about 3 a.m., Mackenzie Lueck arrived at Hatch Park after an airport ride. Her driver later said she entered another vehicle. That meeting became the last confirmed chapter of her life.

Solved / Guilty plea
Read the evidence trail →

The digital break: Phone records connected Lueck to Ayoola Ajayi and helped investigators focus on his Salt Lake City property.

The backyard: Investigators found a disturbed, burned area containing charred human tissue and bone, burned belongings and DNA evidence consistent with Lueck.

The second location: Her remains were later recovered from a shallow grave in Logan Canyon. Ajayi ultimately pleaded guilty to aggravated murder and admitted he had decided to kill her before their meeting.

Missing Person / Virginia

Mamta Kafle Bhatt: The Search for Answers

Open full case file →

Mamta Kafle Bhatt vanished in 2024. Her remains have never been recovered—but in November 2026, a Virginia jury reached a verdict without a body.

2026 update / Guilty verdict
What convinced the jury? →

The case without a body: Prosecutors built their case from the timeline, evidence and testimony surrounding Mamta’s disappearance from her Manassas Park home.

The defendant testified: Naresh Bhatt denied killing his wife but testified that he found her dead and later dismembered her. The jury convicted him of first-degree murder and related offenses.

What remains unanswered: Mamta’s remains have still not been found. Sentencing is scheduled for March 2027.

Family Murder / Colorado

Chris Watts: The Story the Evidence Undid

Open full case file →

Chris Watts stood before television cameras asking for his missing family to come home. Behind that public plea, investigators were already testing a story that would collapse within days.

Solved / Life sentences
How the story collapsed →

The contradiction: Investigators compared Watts’ account with surveillance, interviews and the rapidly developing timeline.

The confession: Watts eventually admitted killing Shanann, Bella and Celeste. Their bodies were recovered from an oil-site property connected to his work.

The question that still grips audiences: How could a seemingly ordinary family unravel into one of America’s most notorious family murder cases?

Cold Case / Los Angeles

Sherri Rasmussen: DNA Reopened a 1986 Murder

Open full case file →

Sherri Rasmussen was killed in her Los Angeles home in 1986. For years, the case looked like a burglary gone violently wrong—until old evidence told a different story.

Cold case solved
The DNA that changed everything →

The overlooked evidence: Biological evidence preserved from the crime scene was reexamined years later as DNA technology advanced.

The new direction: The forensic results helped investigators focus on Stephanie Lazarus, an LAPD detective who had previously been involved with Rasmussen’s husband.

The outcome: Lazarus was arrested decades after the murder and later convicted, turning a long-dormant case into a landmark example of cold-case forensics.

Missing Person / Aruba

Natalee Holloway: A Disappearance That Spanned Decades

Open full case file →

Natalee Holloway disappeared during a 2005 graduation trip to Aruba. No body was recovered, and for years the central question remained the same: what happened after she left a nightclub?

Long-running disappearance
Why the case lasted decades →

The final known hours: Holloway was last seen leaving with Joran van der Sloot and two other men.

Years of uncertainty: Searches, arrests, releases and competing accounts kept the investigation in the public eye while her remains remained missing.

A later admission: In 2023, van der Sloot admitted in a U.S. court-related proffer that he killed Holloway, an account her mother said finally answered the central question even though her body has never been recovered.

Murder Investigation / Florida

Jared Bridegan: An Ambush on a Dark Road

Open full case file →

A tire was sitting in the road. Jared Bridegan stopped to move it. His two-year-old daughter was still strapped into the vehicle when he was shot to death.

Active prosecutions / 2026
Why police called it an ambush →

The setup: Authorities said the tire was deliberately placed in the road to force Bridegan to stop.

The prosecution: Mario Fernandez Saldana was convicted in August 2026 of first-degree murder and solicitation in connection with the killing.

Still unfolding: Shanna Gardner and Henry Tenon still face charges and are scheduled for a joint trial in 2027. They are presumed innocent unless convicted.

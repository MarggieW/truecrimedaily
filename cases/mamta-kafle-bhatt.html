Mamta Kafle Bhatt: A Murder Case Without a Recovered Body

Mamta Kafle Bhatt, a 28-year-old nurse from Nepal, disappeared in 2024. Her remains have never been recovered. On Nov. 10, 2026, a Virginia jury convicted her husband, Naresh Bhatt, of first-degree murder and offenses involving concealment and defilement of her body.

Disappearance and investigation

Mamta stopped communicating in late July 2024. Investigators focused on the family home and on Naresh Bhatt’s activities surrounding the disappearance. The case ultimately proceeded without recovery of Mamta’s body.

The saw

One of the prosecution’s most significant forensic exhibits was a saw that Bhatt had given to a landscaper. Testing found blood/DNA evidence consistent with Mamta’s profile. Reporting on court filings said the probability of an unrelated match was below one in eight billion. Additional hairs and fragments were recovered from the saw during later forensic work.

Bedroom evidence

Forensic testimony addressed blood evidence from carpet in the couple’s bedroom. DNA analysis strongly supported Mamta and the couple’s daughter as contributors to a mixture. The defense challenged what the evidence could establish about how or when blood came to be there.

Purchases, phones and vehicle movements

Prosecutors presented surveillance footage of Bhatt buying trash bags and cleaning supplies around the disappearance. Phone and vehicle evidence was used to reconstruct movements, including trips associated with trash compactors. These records formed part of the circumstantial timeline presented to jurors.

What Bhatt told the jury

Bhatt denied murdering Mamta but testified that he found her dead, panicked, dismembered her and disposed of her remains. He described a possible overdose/suicide scenario. The jury rejected the defense account and returned guilty verdicts after the multiweek trial.

Relationship evidence

Jurors also heard evidence about marital conflict. Mamta’s brother testified that she had sent him an image appearing to show an injury and said she felt unsafe. This testimony was part of the prosecution’s broader presentation; allegations of prior abuse should be distinguished from the offenses adjudicated at trial.

What remains unresolved

Mamta’s remains have not been found. That means the location of her remains remains a major unanswered question even after the murder conviction. Sentencing is scheduled for March 2027.

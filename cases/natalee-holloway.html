Natalee Holloway: Disappearance, Extortion and a 2023 Admission

Natalee Holloway disappeared in Aruba in 2005. Eighteen years later, Joran van der Sloot pleaded guilty in U.S. federal court to extorting her mother and, as part of his agreement, provided information about Natalee’s death.

The disappearance

Holloway disappeared while on a high-school graduation trip to Aruba. Her last known movements placed her with Joran van der Sloot and others. Searches and investigations continued for years, but her remains were not recovered.

The extortion scheme

In 2010, van der Sloot solicited $250,000 from Beth Holloway, promising to reveal the location of Natalee’s remains and circumstances of her death. According to the U.S. Justice Department, he received $25,100 and supplied information he later characterized as worthless.

The U.S. prosecution

Van der Sloot was temporarily transferred from Peru to Alabama to face federal extortion and wire-fraud charges. On Oct. 18, 2023, he pleaded guilty and received a 20-year federal sentence.

The admission

As part of the plea arrangement, van der Sloot agreed to provide full, accurate and truthful information regarding Natalee’s disappearance. The agreement produced an admission about killing her, giving the family an answer to the central question even though her remains remain missing.

What the conviction was—and was not

The 2023 U.S. conviction was for extortion and wire fraud, not for Natalee’s homicide. That distinction matters: the admission was provided in connection with the federal fraud case. The Justice Department’s case held him accountable for exploiting Beth Holloway’s search for information.

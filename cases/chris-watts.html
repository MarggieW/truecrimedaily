Family Murder / Colorado / Solved

Chris Watts: How the Missing-Family Story Collapsed

Shanann Watts and daughters Bella and Celeste were reported missing on Aug. 13, 2018. Within days, the investigation moved from a missing-person search to homicide—and Chris Watts’s public account unraveled against surveillance, interviews and physical evidence.

The disappearance

Shanann returned home from a trip in the early morning. A friend became alarmed when she could not reach her and contacted authorities. Watts initially said he did not know where his family was.

The house and surveillance

Investigators examined the family home, digital systems and neighborhood surveillance. A neighbor’s camera captured Watts’s truck backing into the driveway in the early morning and later leaving. Investigators also documented the home security system and unusual details surrounding Shanann’s phone, belongings and wedding ring.

Interviews, polygraph and changing account

Watts was interviewed repeatedly and underwent a polygraph examination. His explanations changed. He first gave investigators an account blaming Shanann for the girls’ deaths, then admitted killing Shanann. He later admitted responsibility for killing all three.

Recovery site

The bodies were recovered from an oil work site associated with Watts’s employment. Shanann was buried, while Bella and Celeste were recovered from oil tanks. The recovery transformed the case and supported charges including first-degree murder, unlawful termination of a pregnancy and tampering with deceased human bodies.

Guilty plea and sentence

On Nov. 6, 2018, Watts pleaded guilty to all nine counts. Prosecutors removed the death penalty from consideration with the agreement of Shanann’s family. He was later sentenced to multiple consecutive life terms without parole plus additional years.

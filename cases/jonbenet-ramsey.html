JonBenét Ramsey: Thirty Years of Evidence—and a DNA Decision

JonBenét Ramsey was six when she was reported missing on Dec. 26, 1996 and later found dead inside her Boulder home. Thirty years later, Boulder Police say the homicide remains active and investigators continue interviews, evidence testing and consultation with DNA experts.

The morning of Dec. 26

A ransom note was found inside the Ramsey home and demanded $118,000. Police responded after the family called 911. Hours later, JonBenét’s body was discovered in the basement. Boulder’s official case page lists strangulation as the cause of death; other public records also document significant head trauma.

The scale of the investigation

Boulder Police say detectives have received more than 21,016 tips, letters and emails, traveled to 19 states and spoken with or interviewed more than 1,000 people. The case has been reviewed with federal, state and local partners, including the FBI, Colorado Bureau of Investigation, district attorney and outside forensic laboratories.

DNA: promise and limitation

The public debate increasingly centers on whether newer SNP testing and forensic genetic genealogy can extract useful information from limited biological evidence. The difficulty is that some samples are tiny and complex mixtures. Testing can consume material, so choosing the method and laboratory may be irreversible.

What police say in 2026

Boulder Police say detectives continue following tips, conducting interviews and coordinating with public and private-sector partners. Chief Stephen Redfearn has stressed that releasing investigative details could compromise leads or a future prosecution. District Attorney Michael Dougherty has said advances in forensic analysis give reason for hope, while emphasizing that evidence—not theory—must prove a case.

What is not established

No person has been convicted of JonBenét’s murder. Public theories are not equivalent to evidence. TrueCrimeDaily will label historical suspicions, disputed interpretations and unverified claims as such rather than presenting them as solved facts.
